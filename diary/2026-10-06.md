## What did not go as well

- The second half of the morning did not go as well.
  Familiarity with variance, the Gaussian distribution, and the sampling distribution was mixed.
- The materials avoid expectations and say only "mean" and "sample variance".
  That made them harder for me to teach from: it is not the way I think.

## Follow-up

- Notebook 1 introduces "the bin *edges*, the boundaries between bars"
  (section "A histogram is the count table, drawn")
  without first saying what a bin is.
  Define it there: a bin is the range of values that one bar counts.
- Where notebook 2 meets the three mixed concepts:
  variance gets one sentence, at the `.std()` step;
  "Gaussian" never appears (the notebook says "bell" and "normally distributed");
  and "sampling distribution" is never named,
  although the histograms of 10000 averages are one.
- Notebook 2 uses one word for two kinds of quantity:
  `sigma = 0.5` is the true standard deviation of one toss (a property of the distribution),
  while `means.std()` and "the average of the squares is the variance" describe 10000 simulated averages (a sample).
  Writing the true quantities as expectations, $E[X]$ and $\mathrm{Var}(X) = E[(X - E[X])^2]$,
  and the computed ones as the sample mean and sample variance, would keep the two apart.
